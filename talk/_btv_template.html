Twenty minutes later

It comes. It is not your pizza.

You snap a photo of the bag, you say the order is wrong, and you ask for your money back. Standard Tuesday night.

Nobody at the company is going to open that photo. Nine times out of ten the first thing that reads it is a model, and it has four seconds to make up its mind.

That is already how most refund messages get answered here. The open question is not whether to automate it. The open question is who checks the answers.

Three ways this can go

Somebody has to call it.

💰refund you ❌reject the claim 👤pass it to a human

Refund a claim that was never true and the company bleeds. Reject a real one and you lose that customer for good. Send everything to a person and there was no point automating anything.

Making the call is cheap. Knowing whether the call was any good is the expensive part, and that is the whole talk.

stakes Keep it light. The point is that all three answers are defensible for some orders, which is exactly why grading them is hard.

Word number one

What people mean by an agent 🤖

💬 a model

Text goes in, text comes out. One question, one answer, and then it forgets you completely.

🤖 an agent

A model that is allowed to take steps on its own. It looks things up, calls your systems, decides, and acts without checking in. Sending money is an action.

The difference that matters tonight: a model gives you an answer, an agent gives you a trail of choices, and every single one of them can be wrong by itself.

definition Half this room builds these for a living and half have never written a prompt. Say it once, plainly, and do not apologise for defining it.

So who checks the agent? 👀

Two thousand refunds a day. Nobody is reading two thousand refund letters.

So we hand the job to another model. We paste in the trace, we ask it to score the decision out of ten, and that number goes straight onto a slide.

pivot This is the turn of the talk. Slow right down here.

Word number two

LLM as a judge ⚖️

A second model reads what the first one did and grades it. Cheap, instant, and it never gets tired or grumpy on a Friday evening.

what you send it

the photo, the order, the policy, and the reply the agent wrote

what comes back

a number out of ten, and a very convincing paragraph explaining the number

This is the default way teams evaluate agents now. It is in the tooling, it is in the dashboards, and almost nobody checks it before trusting it.

definition Name the fact that the explanation paragraph is extremely persuasive and completely unverified.

The claim

Your judge is
probably lying
to you.

Not on purpose. It hands you a confident number, in the right format, on time, and that number does not mean what you think it means.

thesis Let the slide sit for a beat before you talk over it.

How to think about it

A judge is an instrument. Instruments get calibrated.

🚫 nobody would accept this

A thermometer that reads differently every time. A weighing scale that quietly skips the heavy things. A ruler that agrees with you mostly because everything you measure is the same length.

😕 but we accept all of it

Every one of those is a real failure in a real judge, and you are going to see all three tonight, live in this room, with no terminal and no API key.

Everything after this slide is one question asked four ways. Does this instrument deserve the number it is printing?

framing The instrument metaphor does a lot of work. Say it once and then stop explaining it.

Why I care about this one

When a model saw something that was not there 👀

I work on multimodal safety. My models look at a picture and decide whether to help. One of them refused, and explained itself beautifully: it named the dangerous object and said where it was sitting.

There was no object. I had removed it. The model was refusing something that existed only inside its own sentence, and my evaluation scored that refusal as correct, because it read like a very good refusal.

💡 what that taught me

My scoring never checked whether the model was right. It checked whether the model sounded right. Every number I had published measured writing quality, and I was calling it safety.

Since then I build the instrument before I trust the reading. That habit is what I am handing over tonight.

Clue one  //  the thermometer 🌡️

Same photo, different score.

I sent the same six hundred orders to the same judge, same prompt, three times. Temperature at zero. Nothing changed except the clock.

0% of orders got the same score all three times
0% changed side of the pass line between passes
0.0 pts average gap between the highest and lowest pass

If you scored your agent once, about a quarter of your pass and fail labels were a coin toss.

clue one Temperature zero is not determinism. Batching, routing and kernel nondeterminism all move the number. People assume this is solved. It is not.

Clue two  //  the scale that skips things ⚖️

The orders the judge never scored.

Twenty two percent came back blank, refused, or in a shape nothing could parse. Your pipeline drops those rows and reports the rest.

It goes quiet on exactly the orders you needed it for, and the number you publish comes from the easy ones.

Clue three  //  the ruler that always agrees 📏

Agreement that counts lucky guesses.

Seventy five percent agreement sounds like a working instrument. A judge that approves every single order, reading nothing at all, scores fifty seven.

Take out the agreement you would get by luck and what is left is 0.50. Moderate. Moderate is not a gate.

Report the chance corrected number with the always approve baseline next to it, or the raw number will flatter you every single time.

clue three Do not say kappa first. Say the idea, then say the name once, then move on.

Now put it back in the agent

Six steps, and the wobble multiplies.

One step held its verdict seventy three times in a hundred. That sounded survivable.

Across all six steps of one pizza refund, fifteen orders in a hundred come out the same way twice.

Nobody grades one step. We grade agents, and agents are made of steps.

compounding This is the slide the engineers photograph. Give it room.

The number a team can act on

One version in four, named wrong.

Two versions of the refund agent. One of them really is better. You score a hundred orders on each and you ship the winner.

At a hundred orders you name the wrong version one time in four.

Nobody can act on a correlation. Everybody can act on that sentence, including the person who signs off your headcount.

decision risk Translate every eval metric into the decision it is used for. This is how you get budget for eval work.

Monday morning

Five things, one afternoon. ☕

  • Score everything three timesPublish the spread next to the average. If the spread embarrasses you, that is information.
  • Count what came back blankBreak the blanks down by how hard the case was. Never quietly drop a row.
  • Take out the luck before you celebratePrint the always approve baseline beside every agreement number you report.
  • Add a second judge that answers yes or noWas this the right call, not was this nicely written. Require both to pass.
  • Say what your eval size buys youTurn the metric into a sentence about how often you ship the wrong thing.
