Twenty minutes later

It arrives, and it is wrong.

A customer photographs the bag, says the meal is wrong, and asks for their money back.

Nobody at the company is going to look at that photo. Roughly nine times out of ten, the first thing that reads it is a model.

Real platforms in this region already answer most refund messages automatically. The only open question is who checks the answers.

the case Everything later in the talk is this one order. Say the amount out loud once so it feels like money: twenty two dollars.

Three ways this can go

Somebody has to decide.

refund the customer reject the claim send it to a person

Refund a claim that was never true and the company bleeds money. Reject a real one and the customer is gone forever. Send everything to a person and there is no point automating anything.

The decision is cheap. Knowing whether the decision was any good is the expensive part, and that is the whole talk.

stakes Keep it light. The point is only that all three answers are defensible for some orders, which is exactly why grading is hard.

Word number one

What people mean by an agent

a model

You send text in, you get text back. One question, one answer, and then it forgets you.

an agent

A model that is allowed to take steps. It can look things up, call your systems, decide, and act, all without checking in. Refunding money is an action.

The difference that matters tonight is simple. A model produces an answer. An agent produces a trail of choices, and every one of them can be wrong on its own.

definition Some of the room builds these for a living and some have never written a prompt. Say it once, plainly, and do not apologise for defining it.

So who checks the agent?

Two thousand refunds a day. Nobody is reading two thousand refund letters.

So we hand the job to another model. We paste in the trace, we ask it to score the decision out of ten, and we put that number in a slide.

pivot This is the turn. Slow down here.

Word number two

LLM as a judge

A second model reads what the first one did and grades it. Cheap, instant, and it never gets tired or bored on a Friday afternoon.

what you send it

the photo, the order, the policy, and the reply the agent wrote

what it sends back

a number out of ten, and a paragraph explaining the number

This is now the default way teams evaluate agents. It is in the tooling, it is in the dashboards, and almost nobody validates it before trusting it.

definition Name that the explanation paragraph is very persuasive and completely unverified.

The claim

Your judge is
probably lying
to you.

Not out of malice. It gives you a confident number, in the right format, on time, and the number does not mean what you think it means.

thesis Let the slide sit for a beat before you speak over it.

How to think about it

A judge is an instrument, and instruments get calibrated.

nobody would accept this

A thermometer that reads differently every time you use it. A scale that quietly skips the heavy items. A ruler that agrees with you mostly because everything you measure is the same length.

but we accept all of it

Every one of those is a real failure in a real judge, and I am going to show you each of them tonight, in this room, with no terminal and no API key.

Everything after this slide is one question asked four different ways: does this instrument deserve the number it is printing?

framing The instrument metaphor is doing a lot of work. Say it once and then stop explaining it.

Why I care about this one

The first time a model saw something that was not there

I work on multimodal safety. My models look at a picture and decide whether to help. One of them refused a request and explained itself beautifully: it named the dangerous object in the image, described where it sat, and declined.

There was no such object. I had already removed it. The model was refusing something that existed only in its own sentence, and my evaluation scored that refusal as correct, because it read like a very good refusal.

what that taught me

My scoring never checked whether the model was right. It checked whether the model sounded right. Every number I had published measured writing quality and I was calling it safety.

Since then I build the instrument before I trust the reading. That habit is what I am handing over tonight.

personal This is the emotional centre of the talk. Slow, first person, no hedging. Tie it straight back to the refund: a judge that grades prose will love a confident wrong refund too.

Clue one  //  the thermometer

Same photo, different score.

I sent the same six hundred orders to the same judge, with the same prompt, three times. Temperature at zero. Nothing changed but the clock.

0% of orders got the same score all three times
0% changed side of the pass line between passes
0.0 pts average spread between the highest and lowest pass

If you scored your agent once, roughly a quarter of your pass and fail labels were a coin toss.

clue one Temperature zero is not determinism. Batching, routing and kernel nondeterminism all move the number. People assume this is solved and it is not.

Clue two  //  the scale that skips things

The orders the judge never scored.

Twenty two percent of orders came back blank, refused, or in a shape nothing could parse. Your pipeline drops those rows and reports the rest.

One percent of the clear photos went blank. Fifty seven percent of the barely readable ones did.

It goes quiet on exactly the orders you needed it for, and the number you publish comes from the easy ones.

clue two This is survivorship bias with a dropna in the middle of it. Ask who has ever written that line. Everyone has.

Clue three  //  the ruler that always agrees

Agreement that counts lucky guesses.

Seventy five percent agreement sounds like a working instrument. A judge that approves every single order, reading nothing at all, scores fifty seven.

Once you subtract the agreement you would get by luck, what is left is 0.50. That is moderate, and moderate is not a gate.

Report the chance corrected number with the always approve baseline printed next to it, or the raw number will flatter you every time.

clue three Do not say kappa first. Say the idea, then say the name once, then move on.

Clue four  //  the expensive one

Confident, beautifully written, and wrong.

A refund letter that is warm, clear, apologises properly, and gives away twenty two dollars for a photo of the correct meal.

0% of confident wrong refunds got approved
0% after a second judge asks: was this the right call?

A score out of ten answers one question: was this handled well? It is never asked whether it was correct.

Now put it back in the agent

Six steps, and the wobble multiplies.

One step held its verdict seventy three times in a hundred. That sounded survivable.

Across all six steps of a single refund, fifteen orders in a hundred come out the same way twice.

Nobody grades one step. We grade agents, and agents are made of steps.

compounding This is the slide the engineers in the room photograph. Give it room.

The number a team can act on

One version in four, named wrong.

Two versions of the refund agent. One of them really is better. You score a hundred orders on each and you ship the winner.

At a hundred orders you name the wrong version one time in four.

Nobody can act on a correlation. Everybody can act on that sentence, including the person who decides your headcount.

decision risk Translate every eval metric into the decision it is used for. This is how you get budget for eval work.

Monday morning

Five things, one afternoon.

  • Score everything three timesPublish the spread next to the average. If the spread embarrasses you, that is information.
  • Count what came back blankBreak the blanks down by how hard the case was. Never quietly drop a row.
  • Subtract luck before you celebratePrint the always approve baseline beside every agreement number you report.
  • Add a second judge that answers yes or noWas this the right call, not was this well written. Require both to pass.
  • Say what your eval size buys youTurn the metric into a sentence about how often you ship the wrong thing.
takeaway Tell them the repo does all five on their own CSV. This is the slide people photograph.
